How the amounts are worked out

Pick a method, then enter the water you will use (or the coffee you have, or how many cups you want) and the ratio. Coffee is water ÷ ratio: 500 ml of water at 1:16 needs 31 g of coffee. Going the other way, 30 g of coffee at 1:15 in a French press takes 450 ml of water. Water can be measured in millilitres or grams, since a millilitre of water weighs a gram; a kitchen scale under the brewer is the easiest way to pour accurately.

Not all the water reaches the cup: spent grounds keep about 2 g of water per gram of coffee, so those 500 ml give about 440 ml of coffee. Espresso is the exception: its ratio is measured on the beverage weight, so 18 g of coffee at 1:2 means 36 g of espresso in the cup.

Frequently asked questions

How many grams of coffee per cup?

For pour-over, about 15-16 g of coffee per 250 ml (8.5 fl oz) of water, a 1:16 ratio. The Specialty Coffee Association's Golden Cup reference is 55 g per litre, or 8.25 g per 150 ml, slightly milder. French press is usually a bit stronger, around 17 g per 250 ml.

What does a 1:16 coffee ratio mean?

One gram of coffee for every 16 grams (or millilitres) of water. 30 g of coffee to 480 ml of water is 1:16. The smaller the second number, the stronger the brew.

How much coffee for a 12-cup coffee maker?

A «cup» on a drip machine is usually 5 to 6 fl oz, so 12 cups is roughly 1.8 litres of water. At 1:16 that is about 110 g of coffee, about 4 oz. Use the «Cups» option with your machine's cup size.

What is the espresso brew ratio?

A double espresso typically uses 16-20 g of coffee and yields 32-40 g in the cup (1:2) in about 25-30 seconds. A ristretto stops earlier (1:1.5) and a lungo runs longer (1:3). Weigh the drink, not the water.

What ratio for cold brew?

Coarse coffee and cold water at about 1:8 for a concentrate, 12 to 24 hours in the fridge, strained and served cut 1:1 with water or milk. For ready-to-drink cold brew, use 1:15 or 1:16.
