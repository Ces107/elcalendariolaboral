How much paint for a room

Take the wall area, multiply by the number of coats and divide by the coverage printed on the can. A 12 × 10 ft bedroom with 8 ft ceilings has 352 sq ft of wall; take away one door (about 20 sq ft) and one window (about 15 sq ft) and 317 sq ft are left. At 350 sq ft per gallon and two coats that is 1.81 gallons, or 1.99 gallons with a 10% spare: buy two one-gallon cans.

In metric the same logic applies. A 4 × 3 m room with 2.5 m walls, a door and a window gives 31.9 m²; two coats of a 10 m²/L emulsion need 7.0 litres including the spare, so a 5 L and a 2.5 L tin, or a single 10 L tub if you will also do touch-ups later. Switch the units button at the top to change between feet and gallons and metres and litres.

Coverage: the can versus the wall

Coverage (also called spreading rate) is the area one gallon or one litre covers in a single coat. Typical interior latex or emulsion paint states 350 to 400 sq ft per gallon, or 10 to 14 m² per litre. For context, the EU Ecolabel requires white and light indoor paints to reach at least 8 m² per litre while hiding 98% of the surface beneath, so treat much higher claims as ideal laboratory conditions.

Real walls take more. Fresh plaster and bare drywall soak up the first coat (the "New plaster" option adds 15%), and textured finishes such as knockdown, popcorn or pebble-dash have more surface than a tape measure shows (+30%). Covering a dark colour with a light one usually needs a primer plus two coats, or three coats of a one-coat paint, whatever the label says.

What the calculator counts

Walls are the room's perimeter times its height. Each door subtracts 20 sq ft (1.6 m²) and each window 15 sq ft (1.5 m²): typical sizes, so use the "Area" mode with your own measurement for patio doors or picture windows. The ceiling is length times width. Sockets, switches and vents are not subtracted; the spare covers them, plus what stays in the roller and the tray.

The can suggestion picks the cheapest mix, assuming paint gets cheaper per gallon or litre in bigger containers: 5-gallon buckets, gallons and quarts in US mode; 10, 5, 2.5 and 1 litre tins in metric. Buy tinted paint from a single batch, because two mixes of the same colour can look different side by side on one wall.

Frequently asked questions

How many square feet does a gallon of paint cover?

About 350 to 400 sq ft in one coat on a smooth, primed wall. On new drywall, bare plaster or texture expect 250 to 300 sq ft.

How many coats of paint do I need?

Two for almost every job. One coat is enough only when you repaint the same colour on a sound wall; a big colour change or fresh plaster needs a primer first.

Do I subtract doors and windows?

Yes, for the large openings. The calculator takes 20 sq ft (1.6 m²) per door and 15 sq ft (1.5 m²) per window. Small items such as outlets are left in.

How much paint for a ceiling?

Its area is the room's length times its width. A 12 × 10 ft ceiling is 120 sq ft: about two-thirds of a gallon for two coats.

How much spare paint should I buy?

Around 10% for brush and roller work, 20 to 30% if you spray, because part of the paint is lost as overspray.
