How your zones are worked out

Heart rate zones come from two numbers: your maximum heart rate and, for the Karvonen method, your resting heart rate. Karvonen works on the heart-rate reserve, max minus resting: target HR = resting + (max − resting) × intensity. Two people with the same max but different fitness get different zones, which is why it is more accurate than taking a straight percentage of max.

Example: at 35 the Tanaka formula estimates a max of 208 − 0.7 × 35 = 183.5 bpm. With a resting rate of 60 bpm the reserve is 123.5, so zone 2 (60-70%) runs from 134 to 146 bpm. With the plain percentage-of-max method the same zone would be 110-128 bpm, noticeably lower. That gap is why it pays to measure your resting pulse (lying down, just after waking, over a full minute, on several mornings) and use Karvonen.

Which max heart rate formula

The familiar 220 − age spread through fitness books in the 1970s without a proper study behind it; it tends to overestimate max heart rate in young people and underestimate it in older ones. Tanaka and colleagues re-examined it in 2001 using 351 studies covering about 19,000 people and proposed 208 − 0.7 × age, now the usual default. For women, Gulati's 2010 study of more than 5,400 women found 206 − 0.88 × age. All of them are averages, and an individual's real maximum can sit 10 bpm either side. If you have measured yours in a lab test or an all-out effort with a chest strap, enter it as "Measured max HR" and it replaces the formula.

What each zone is for

Zone 1 (50-60%) is for warming up and recovery. Zone 2 (60-70%) is the aerobic base where you can still talk in full sentences, and where most plans put the bulk of weekly volume. Zone 3 (70-80%) is a steady, comfortably hard effort close to marathon pace. Zone 4 (80-90%) sits around threshold, with heavy breathing: 3 to 10 minute intervals or 10K pace. Zone 5 (90-100%) can only be held for a few minutes. If you take blood-pressure or heart medication (beta blockers lower heart rate) or have a heart condition, ask your doctor for your zones: these formulas do not apply.

Frequently asked questions

What is my max heart rate for my age?

With Tanaka's formula, 208 − 0.7 × age: 187 bpm at 30, 180 at 40, 173 at 50 and 166 at 60. It is an average with about ±10 bpm of spread.

What heart rate is zone 2?

60-70% of your heart-rate reserve (or of max). For a 35-year-old with a resting rate of 60, that is about 134-146 bpm with Karvonen.

Karvonen or percentage of max?

Karvonen if you know your resting heart rate, because it accounts for fitness. Percentage of max is simpler but gives lower zones.

How do I measure resting heart rate?

In the morning before getting up, count your pulse for a full minute (or read your watch) on several days and take the average.

Why does my watch show different zones?

Each brand uses its own formula and percentages. What matters is sticking to one system with a realistic maximum.
