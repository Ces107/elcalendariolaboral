How the numbers are worked out

Sugar is a percentage of the prepared fruit weight (pitted, peeled, stems off): 1 kg of strawberries at 75 % takes 750 g of sugar. To estimate the yield, the calculator adds the sugar you put in to the sugar already in the fruit (4.9 g per 100 g of strawberries in USDA FoodData Central) and finds the weight at which that sugar is 60 % of the jam: (750 + 49) ÷ 0.60 = 1,332 g of jam. Getting there means boiling off 1,750 − 1,332 = 418 g of water, almost a quarter of what is in the pot.

A 1:1 jam barely needs reducing and sets fast; with less sugar you cook longer, and below about 50 % you usually need pectin (or pectin-rich fruit such as apple or quince) to set without cooking the fruit to paste. Lemon juice is not only for flavour: acidity helps pectin gel. Fruit sugar values are averages; very ripe fruit is sweeter.

Shelf life and sugar

Sugar is part of what preserves jam: at around 60 % it ties up enough water to keep well once opened. Lower-sugar spreads are fine, they just belong in the fridge after opening. The calculator also reports fruit per kilo of finished jam: 1 kg of fruit with 750 g of sugar cooked to 60 % gives 751 g of fruit per kg, comfortably above the 450 g European minimum for jam. Fill hot, sterilized jars, process them as NCHFP describes, and keep opened jars refrigerated.

Frequently asked questions

How much sugar per pound of fruit for jam?

The old rule is 1:1, a pound of sugar per pound of fruit. Most modern recipes use 0.6 to 0.75 lb per lb, and very sweet fruit (figs, mango) or added pectin lets you go down to half. Below that you need pectin made for low-sugar jam and the fridge for storage.

How much jam will I get from my fruit?

With 1 kg (2.2 lb) of strawberries and 750 g of sugar, about 1,330 g of jam cooked to 60 % sugar, or five 250 ml (half-pint) jars. Less sugar means less jam, because more water has to be cooked off before it sets.

How do I know when jam is set?

The National Center for Home Food Preservation gives three tests: a temperature of 220 °F (8 °F above the boiling point of water) at sea level, the spoon test (two drops run together and «sheet» off a cool metal spoon), or a little jam on a plate in the freezer for a few minutes that gels.

How long do I process jam jars in a water bath?

NCHFP: fill hot jars leaving ¼ inch headspace and process 5 minutes in a boiling-water canner if the jars were sterilized, 10 minutes if not, adding a minute per 1,000 ft of altitude to the 5-minute time. Reduced-sugar spreads are best refrigerated or frozen.

What makes a jam legally «jam» in Europe?

Under the EU breakfast directive, as amended by Directive (EU) 2024/1438, jam needs at least 450 g of fruit per kilo of product (500 g for «extra jam») from 14 June 2026, and EU countries such as Spain keep a 60 % soluble-solids minimum, except for reduced-sugar products.
