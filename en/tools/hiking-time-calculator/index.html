Naismith's rule

In 1892 the Scottish climber William W. Naismith set out the rule hillwalkers still learn: allow one hour for every 3 miles (5 km) of distance, plus one hour for every 2,000 feet (600 m) of ascent. The calculator works in metric internally (5 km/h and 600 m per hour), which differs from the feet version by under 2%. An 8-mile loop with 2,500 ft of climbing comes out at 3 h 51 min of walking.

Naismith ignores descent, and long, steep descents are where most people slow down. Eric Langmuir, author of the British mountain-leader handbook, added two corrections: on gentle descents (5 to 12 degrees) take off 10 minutes for every 300 m (about 1,000 ft) of descent; on steep ones (over 12 degrees) add 10 minutes per 300 m. Choose the steep option for the same loop and the estimate grows to 4 h 16 min.

The MIDE method used in Spain

Spanish trail boards and guidebooks use MIDE, the excursion information method of the Spanish mountain sports federation. It is more conservative. Horizontal time runs at 5 km/h on roads and tracks, 4 km/h on good paths and 3 km/h on rough ground; vertical time at 400 m per hour up and 600 m per hour down. You take the longer of the two and add half of the shorter. On a good path the same 8-mile, 2,500 ft loop gives 4 h 48 min, which suits an average walker with a daypack better than the original Naismith figure. MIDE also grades effort from 1 (up to an hour) to 5 (over ten hours).

From walking time to a plan for the day

Both rules give moving time only. Add the breaks you expect (lunch, photos, water) under "More options" and type a start time to see when you should be back, then check that leaves a margin before sunset, especially in winter. Children, heavy packs, snow, heat or poor visibility all slow you down well beyond any formula, so treat the result as a minimum.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to hike 1,000 feet of elevation?

Naismith allows 30 minutes per 1,000 ft of ascent on top of the distance time. MIDE allows about 45 minutes per 1,000 ft (400 m per hour).

What is an average hiking speed?

Around 3 mph (5 km/h) on easy flat ground and 2 to 2.5 mph on mountain paths, slowing further with every 1,000 ft of climbing.

Does Naismith's rule include breaks?

No, it is moving time. Add breaks separately with "Breaks" under "More options"; a common allowance is 10 minutes per hour.

Which method should I use?

Naismith suits fit hillwalkers on good paths. MIDE is more realistic for mixed groups and counts the descent.

What is total ascent?

The sum of every climb on the route (elevation gain), not just the height difference between the start and the summit.
