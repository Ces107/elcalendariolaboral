Margin and markup are not the same

An item that costs you $10, sold at a 40% margin: margin is a share of the selling price, so the price is 10 ÷ (1 − 0.40) = $16.67 and you make $6.67, which is 40% of $16.67. Apply a 40% markup instead and the price is 10 × 1.40 = $14.00: you make $4.00, a margin of only 28.6%. Mixing the two up is the most common pricing mistake in small shops: a 40% markup sounds generous and leaves little.

The conversion never changes: margin = markup ÷ (1 + markup), and markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin). A 50% margin is a 100% markup (selling at twice the cost); a 25% markup is a 20% margin.

With marketplace fees

When a marketplace takes a percentage of the final price, that percentage eats margin too. Keep the $10 item and add 11% in fees (about what Etsy takes in the US on a $30 order): to keep a true 40% margin the price rises to $20.41. Fees are charged on what the customer pays, tax included, so the formula is price = cost ÷ (1 − margin − fee × (1 + tax)). Tick "End in .99" to round the final price up ($16.67 becomes $16.99); the margin shown is recalculated on the rounded price.

Sales tax and VAT

In the US, prices are usually shown before sales tax, so leave tax at 0% and let the checkout add it. In the UK and EU, consumer prices include VAT: a UK business charging 20% VAT needs £20.00 on the shelf for the same 40% margin on a £10 cost, because the VAT part (£3.33) is not yours. Reduced rates exist for some goods (in the UK, 5% for things like children's car seats and 0% for most food and books; HMRC's list). Margin is always worked out on the price before tax.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a selling price from a margin?

Divide the cost by (1 − margin). With a $10 cost and a 30% margin: 10 ÷ 0.70 = $14.29.

What is the difference between margin and markup?

Margin is profit as a share of the selling price; markup is profit as a share of the cost. A 50% margin equals a 100% markup.

Is margin calculated before or after tax?

Before. Sales tax and VAT are collected for the government, so they are not part of your revenue or your margin.

What margin should a handmade product have?

It depends on overheads, but below a 30% true margin after fees it is hard to pay for your time, returns and ads.

How do I turn a margin into a markup?

Markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin). A 40% margin is a 66.7% markup.
