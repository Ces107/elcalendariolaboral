How many sales you need

An online shop with $600 a month in fixed costs (platform subscription, software, insurance, storage) sells an item at $25 that costs $9 to make and ship. Each sale leaves $16 to cover the fixed costs: the contribution margin. 600 ÷ 16 = 37.5, so the shop needs 38 sales a month, about 9 a week, to stop losing money. From sale 39 on, every order adds $16 of profit.

Sell through a marketplace that keeps 13% of the price and the contribution margin drops to $12.75, pushing break-even to 48 sales; earning $400 a month on top takes 79. Raising the price 10% to $27.50, with no fees, cuts break-even to 33 sales: a 10% price change moves the target by 14%.

The formula

Break-even units = fixed costs ÷ (price − variable cost per unit). Break-even revenue = fixed costs ÷ contribution margin ratio, where the ratio is (price − variable cost) ÷ price. In the example, 16 ÷ 25 = 0.64 and 600 ÷ 0.64 = $937.50 of sales a month. For a target profit, add it to the fixed costs: (600 + 400) ÷ margin. This is standard cost-volume-profit analysis, as taught in OpenStax's Principles of Managerial Accounting.

What goes in each box

  • Fixed costs: what you pay in a month even with zero sales: rent, subscriptions, insurance, an accountant, the fixed part of your ad budget.
  • Price: the selling price before sales tax or VAT, which you only collect for the government.
  • Cost per unit: materials, packaging, postage you pay, print-on-demand cost; anything that grows with each sale.
  • Fees: the share of the price that the marketplace or payment processor keeps (Etsy, Amazon, PayPal, Stripe).

Not sure whether a cost is fixed or variable? Ask whether it would double if your sales doubled. If yes, it is variable.

Frequently asked questions

What is the break-even point?

The sales volume at which revenue exactly covers all costs, so you neither make nor lose money. Below it you lose; above it each sale's contribution margin is profit.

How do I calculate break-even units?

Fixed costs ÷ (price − variable cost per unit). With $600 of fixed costs, a $25 price and a $9 cost: 37.5, so 38 units.

Should the price include sales tax or VAT?

No. Use the price before tax: the tax is collected for the government and does not pay your costs.

How can I lower my break-even point?

Raise the price, cut the cost per unit or trim fixed costs. Price usually moves it most, because every extra dollar goes straight to contribution margin.

Does my own salary count as a fixed cost?

If the business is meant to pay you, yes: add it to fixed costs or enter it as the target profit.
