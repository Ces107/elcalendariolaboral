Speed, distance and time

Average speed is total distance divided by total time. Rearranged, time = distance ÷ speed and distance = speed × time. Pick what you want to find, fill in the other two and the calculator answers in mph or km/h, plus metres per second and the pace per mile or kilometre.

Example: a 200-mile drive that takes 3 hours 20 minutes door to door averages 200 ÷ 3.333 = 60 mph, even if you spent most of it at 70 on the motorway; fuel stops, town traffic and roadworks pull the average down. The other way round, 120 miles at an average of 50 mph takes 2.4 hours, which is 2 hours 24 minutes, not 2 hours 40. Type the time as h:mm ("3:20"), as "3h20" or as minutes ("90min"); a plain number is read as hours.

Why you cannot average two speeds

Drive 60 miles out at 40 mph and back at 60 mph and your average is not 50 mph but 48 mph. The outward leg takes 1 h 30 min and the return 1 h, so 120 miles take 2 h 30 min. You spend longer at the slower speed, so it weighs more in the average (it is the harmonic mean, not the arithmetic one). For any multi-leg trip, add up all the distances and all the times and divide once. The same arithmetic explains why making up lost time later in a journey takes a much higher speed than people expect.

Average speed cameras and breaks

Average speed cameras, common on UK motorway roadworks and some rural A-roads, time your car between two points and work out exactly this average. Over a 3-mile section with a 50 mph limit, the minimum legal time is 3 ÷ 50 × 3,600 = 216 seconds, 3 min 36 s; covering it in 3 minutes means 60 mph. Enter the limit under "More options" to see the minimum time and how far above or below the limit you are. National limits for cars on GOV.UK are 30 mph in built-up areas, 60 on single carriageways and 70 on dual carriageways and motorways, unless signs say otherwise.

For long drives, the Highway Code (rule 91) recommends a break of at least 15 minutes after every two hours of driving: at a 60 mph average that is roughly every 120 miles.

Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate average speed?

Divide the total distance by the total time. 150 miles in 2 h 30 min (2.5 h) is 60 mph.

How long does it take to drive 100 miles at 70 mph?

About 1 hour 26 minutes: 100 ÷ 70 = 1.43 h. At 60 mph it would be 1 h 40 min, only 14 minutes more.

How do I convert mph to km/h?

Multiply by 1.609344, because the international mile is exactly 1,609.344 metres. 70 mph is 112.7 km/h.

How do average speed cameras work?

They read your number plate at two or more points and divide the distance between them by the time you took. If that average is above the limit, you can be fined even if you slowed down at the cameras.

Does driving faster save much time?

Less than it seems. Over 200 miles, going 75 instead of 70 mph saves about 11 minutes and burns noticeably more fuel.
