How the scaling works

For servings, the factor is simply servings you need ÷ servings in the recipe: from 4 to 6, × 1.5. For pans, what matters is the surface area. An 8-inch round is 50.3 square inches and a 9-inch round is 63.6, so the factor is 1.27, not 1.125. That is why a cake written for an 8-inch pan comes out thin and dry when baked unchanged in a 9-inch one. Inches or centimetres both work, as long as both pans use the same unit.

Paste the ingredient list and every quantity is multiplied. At × 1.27, a recipe with 1½ cups of flour, 1 cup of sugar, 2 eggs and ½ cup of milk becomes 1.9 cups of flour, 1.27 cups of sugar, 2.53 eggs (call it 2 large plus a yolk) and 0.63 cup of milk. For exact baking, convert the cups to grams first.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert a recipe from an 8-inch to a 9-inch round pan?

Scale by the ratio of the areas, not the diameters: (9 ÷ 8)² = 1.27. A 9-inch round has 27 % more surface than an 8-inch, so multiply every ingredient by about 1.27 for the same depth. From 9 to 10 inches the factor is 1.23.

Can I bake a two-layer 9-inch cake in a 9 × 13 pan?

Almost: two 9-inch rounds are 127 square inches and a 9 × 13 is 117, so the batter fills it a little deeper. It works for most butter cakes; bake at the same temperature and start checking about 10 minutes later than the layer time.

How do I halve a recipe with 3 eggs?

That is 1.5 eggs. Beat two eggs and use three quarters of it, or use one egg plus one yolk. A large US egg is about 50 g without the shell, so you can also weigh 75 g of beaten egg.

Does the baking time change when I scale a recipe?

If the batter depth stays the same, the time changes little. Deeper batter (a smaller pan for the same amount) needs longer, and sometimes 25 °F less so the outside does not overbake before the middle sets. Use a toothpick test.

Can I paste my ingredient list?

Yes: start each ingredient with its quantity and separate them with semicolons («2 1/4 cups flour; 2 eggs; 1/2 tsp salt»). Fractions like 1/2, mixed numbers like 1 1/2 and characters like ½ all work.
