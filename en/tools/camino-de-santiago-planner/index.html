How the planner splits your days

Pick a route and a starting town, type how far you want to walk each day and the date you set off. The planner starts from the classic stages of each Camino and, day by day, picks the stopping point that best fits your daily distance while keeping your running total on track: strong walkers get two short stages merged, gentler walkers get long stages cut into equal parts, with the approximate kilometre where you should look for a bed. "Rest every" adds a day off after a set number of walking days, in whichever town you have reached. Print the plan or download it as an .ics file for your phone's calendar.

A worked example: the full Camino Francés from Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port is about 764 km (475 miles) over 33 classic stages. At 22 km (14 miles) a day the plan follows those stages almost exactly, 33 walking days, or 37 with a rest day every week. At 15 km a day it takes 51 days, with many stages split in two; at 30 km, 26 days. From Sarria it is 114 km, the most popular minimum for the Compostela, usually walked in five days.

The four routes and their distances

The Camino Francés is the busiest route and has the most services. The central Portuguese Way comes up from Lisbon through Coimbra and Porto: 241 km from Porto and 116 km from Tui, just over the border in Galicia and the classic start for the last 100 km. The Camino del Norte follows the Bay of Biscay coast from Irún (821 km) and joins the Francés at Arzúa; it is hillier and has fewer albergues. The Primitivo, the oldest route, leaves Oviedo, crosses the Asturian mountains and meets the Francés at Melide: about 306 km with big daily climbs. The Primitivo plan takes the Hospitales stage (the bad-weather alternative via Pola de Allande adds 4.5 km) and the Norte uses the official variants.

The Compostela and the 100 km minimum

To receive the Compostela certificate at the Pilgrim Office you must walk (or ride a horse) at least the last 100 km into Santiago, or cycle the last 200 km, and collect at least two dated stamps a day in your credential over that final stretch. The planner warns you when your chosen start falls short. Stage distances are approximate and come from the Gronze guide; expect a kilometre or two of difference depending on where you sleep and on any diversions.

Frequently asked questions

How many days does the Camino Francés take?

30 to 35 walking days at 22 to 25 km (14 to 15 miles) a day from Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port, plus any rest days. From Sarria, about five days.

How far do pilgrims walk each day?

Most walk 20 to 25 km (12 to 15 miles). If you are not used to it, keep the first days to 15 to 18 km: blisters and tendinitis come from pushing early.

Where do I start to earn the Compostela?

Anywhere 100 km or more from Santiago on foot: Sarria on the Francés, Tui on the Portuguese Way or Vilalba on the Norte. On the Primitivo, Lugo is right on the limit; O Cádavo gives you a margin.

How often should I take a rest day?

Many pilgrims rest one day a week, often in a city worth seeing: Pamplona, Burgos or León on the Francés.

Are the distances exact?

They are the stage distances published by the Gronze guide. Real distances shift a little with your choice of lodging and with diversions; allow about 5%.

What if a stage is longer than I want to walk?

The planner cuts it into equal parts and gives the approximate kilometre. Look for lodging near that point in a Camino guide or app.
