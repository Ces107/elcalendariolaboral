Gear inches, development and speed

Three numbers describe a bike gear. Gear inches = wheel diameter in inches × chainring teeth ÷ cog teeth, a measure that goes back to the high-wheel bicycles whose front wheel size set the gear. Development (or rollout) is how far the bike travels per crank turn: gear ratio × wheel circumference. Speed is development × cadence. On a 700×25c wheel (about 2111 mm round, 26.5 in across) a 50×11 top gear is 120.2 gear inches, or 9.60 m per pedal stroke, and at 90 rpm that is 32.2 mph. The compact low gear, 34×30, is 30.0 inches, 2.39 m and 8.0 mph at the same cadence.

Getting the wheel size right

Choose the wheel by its ETRTO size, moulded on the tyre sidewall: 25-622 means a 25 mm tyre on a rim with a 622 mm bead seat diameter (700c). The circumference is estimated as π × (622 + 2 × 25) = 2111 mm. For a precise figure, roll it out: mark the tyre and the floor, push the bike one full turn with your weight on it and measure the distance, then type it as "Circumference". A 29×2.35 mountain-bike tyre comes out at about 2331 mm; with a 32-tooth ring and a 51-tooth cog that is a tiny 18.3 gear inches for steep climbs.

Choosing a range

For long climbs, a common rule is to be able to keep 70 to 80 rpm in your lowest gear: climbing at 6 mph that means about 25 to 30 gear inches, which is why compact chainsets with 32 or 34-tooth cogs have become standard on endurance road bikes. On the flat most amateur riders cruise at 18 to 22 mph in gears of 80 to 95 inches. Steps of more than about 15% between neighbouring cogs feel like a gap when you shift; tight cassettes such as 11-28 give smooth steps but less range.

Frequently asked questions

How many gear inches is 50×11?

About 120.2 gear inches on a 700×25c wheel; slightly more on wider 700c tyres.

What low gear do I need for climbing?

Low enough to spin 70 to 80 rpm. For steady climbing at 5 to 6 mph that is roughly 22 to 30 gear inches, such as 34×32 or 34×34 on a road bike.

How do I work out speed from cadence?

Speed (mph) = gear inches × cadence × π × 60 ÷ 63,360. With 90 gear inches at 90 rpm: about 24 mph.

What is the difference between gear inches and development?

Gear inches use the wheel diameter; development (rollout) uses the circumference, so it equals gear inches × π, usually given in metres.

How do I measure my wheel circumference?

Mark the tyre and the floor, roll the bike one full turn while sitting on it and measure the gap in millimetres.
